## AI Image & Multimodal Models: Live Demo + Hands-On

---

## Overview

**Theme:** From Text to Pictures — How AI Sees and Creates
**Format:** Teacher-led demos followed by student hands-on exploration
**Tools Needed:** ChatGPT (chat.openai.com), Gemini (gemini.google.com), or any AI with image generation

---

## Demo Schedule

### Demo 1: Same Prompt, Different Tools (~10 min)

**Setup:** Teacher shares screen with two AI tools open side by side.

**Prompt to use on both:**
> "A robot reading a book in a cozy library, soft warm lighting, digital art style"

**Discussion points:**
- How did the two results differ?
- Which one followed the prompt more closely?
- Did either one get something "wrong"?

---

### Demo 2: Vague vs. Detailed Prompt (~10 min)

**Round 1 (vague):** "Draw a cat"
**Round 2 (detailed):** "A fluffy orange tabby cat sleeping on a windowsill, raindrops on the glass behind it, Studio Ghibli anime style, peaceful blue and gray color palette"

**Discussion points:**
- How much better is the detailed version?
- What specific details made the biggest difference?
- Connect to RTCF: subject = task, style = format, setting = context

---

### Demo 3: Multimodal — Upload & Ask (~10 min)

**Setup:** Teacher uploads a photo (classroom, school hallway, or nature photo) to ChatGPT or Gemini.

**Questions to ask the AI:**
1. "What do you see in this image?"
2. "What room is this? How can you tell?"
3. "What time of day do you think this was taken?"

**Then try a tricky one:**
- Upload handwritten notes and ask AI to read them
- Upload a meme and ask AI to explain the joke

**Discussion:** What was easy for the AI? What was hard?

---

### Demo 4: Spot the Fake — Real vs. AI (~10 min)

**Setup:** Teacher prepares 5-6 images in advance (mix of real photos and AI-generated). Show one at a time and have students vote: REAL or AI?

**Where to find AI-generated images for the activity:**
- Generate some with DALL-E/ChatGPT before class
- Use websites like "Which Face Is Real?" (whichfaceisreal.com)
- Search for known AI-generated viral images

**Tips to point out:**
- Hands/fingers (often wrong)
- Text in images (garbled)
- Background inconsistencies
- "Too perfect" skin or lighting
- Check the source!

---

### Demo 5: The Ethics Moment (~5 min)

**Ask the class:** "Could I make a fake image of [celebrity] doing something they never did? Should I?"

**Key rules to establish:**
1. NEVER make fake images of real people
2. Always tell people if an image is AI-generated
3. Don't trust images online without checking the source
4. Think about how an image could hurt someone before sharing

---

## Student Hands-On (Classwork Time)

After demos, students work on the classwork PDF independently:
- Section 1: Their own vague vs. detailed prompt battle
- Section 2: Upload a photo and test multimodal AI
- Section 3: Judge the images from the class demo (Real vs. AI)
- Section 4: Ethics reflection

## AI Connection (per CLAUDE.md)

This entire lesson IS the AI connection — students directly interact with cutting-edge AI image generation and multimodal understanding. The connection to prior weeks:
- **Prompting (RTCF):** Translates directly to image prompts (subject=task, style=format, mood=context)
- **Hallucination:** AI images can also "hallucinate" — extra fingers, impossible physics
- **Agents:** Image generation is a TOOL an AI agent could use
- **Ethics:** Deepfakes are the image equivalent of text hallucinations — but potentially more harmful
